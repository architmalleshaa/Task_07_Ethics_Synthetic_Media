# Stress test and residual risks

The policy governs a small teaching lab with an editor and a faculty director. It cannot directly bind strangers, platforms or every person who receives a downloaded file. Its claims should stay within that operational boundary.

## A well-intentioned editor shortens a clip

An editor may copy an approved answer into a social post and assume the original approval is sufficient. That action would breach the excerpt rule, but the rule itself does not prevent the edit. A release register and second reviewer make the mistake more visible only when the employee follows the workflow. Periodic checks of actual public files can find some discrepancies after release; they cannot guarantee that nobody heard an unlabeled copy first.

## The data are correct but the recommendation is unfair

A reviewer may verify every number and still miss that “largest headroom” is being treated as evidence of the weakest player. The formula conditions on a selected shooting target, a minimum number of attempts and no shot-quality adjustment. Correct arithmetic does not settle whether that framing is fair. The policy requires inferential review, but that depends on reviewer competence and sensitivity to context. An additional subject-matter review can reduce this risk without making it disappear.

## Consent is documented but not freely given

A student may believe that refusing their own modeled voice will disappoint a supervisor even when the form says consent is voluntary. A signature alone does not establish freedom from pressure. An alternative assignment, independent contact and meaningful ability to revoke would help. The lab still relies partly on people reporting concerns and on supervisors behaving honestly.

## A bad-faith distributor removes the context

Someone can crop the spoken opening, remove metadata and attach a false caption to a copy. The lab can preserve the original, correct its own channels and request removal, but cannot recall every copy. The actual Task 6 metadata-removal test supports the concern about ordinary tags. It does not quantify how often deceptive re-sharing happens. Signed provenance, where genuinely available and checked, may help someone verify an original; it cannot force an adversary to distribute that original or force an audience to inspect it.

## Production grows beyond the review capacity

The five-variant work-in-progress cap is an operational choice, not a measured optimal threshold. Staff may evade it by treating translations as separate projects or shifting work outside lab accounts. The scope explicitly includes contractors and excerpts, but enforcement still requires access to real release records and leadership willing to delay work. If the team cannot sustain independent listening and factual review, the correct response under this policy is to pause production rather than waive the checks.

## The tools change

The prototype used stock macOS voices. A future system may support cloning, richer emotional delivery or signed credentials. That would change the relevant risks and may require stricter requirements. This submission contains no comparative evidence about those systems and no estimate of detector accuracy. The semester review and incident-triggered update reduce the chance of a stale policy but do not predict new failure modes.

## Limits of this submission

No real employee was interviewed, no organizational adoption was obtained, and no current legal or platform-policy text was consulted. The assignment briefs, permitted datasets, Task 5 calculations, actual Task 6 files/logs and invented scenarios are the source boundary. The policy is an operational proposal, not a claim about existing Syracuse rules or legal compliance. Real deployment would need an authorized organizational owner and applicable legal and platform review.

Perceptual listening review was not performed. The Task 6 evaluation is therefore incomplete in that dimension, and this policy would block public release of those files until a qualified listener checks them. Admitting that limitation is preferable to treating successful generation or metadata inspection as evidence of audible fidelity.

Some reliance on good faith is unavoidable in an internal process: reviewers must actually listen, consent must actually be voluntary and records must actually reflect the released file. A system that assumes everyone is malicious cannot function as a research lab, but a system that assumes honesty is sufficient invites complacency. The policy makes obligations observable where possible and names the residual uncertainty where control ends.
