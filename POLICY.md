# Synthetic audio policy for a university sports analytics teaching lab

**Status:** Proposed policy for a hypothetical teaching lab, not an adopted Syracuse University policy. The assumed team is a faculty director, a communications editor and four student researchers. It publishes retrospective sports analysis for classmates and campus audiences. The following requirements govern its own production and distribution.

**Purpose:** Allow accurate, clearly attributed educational narration while refusing impersonation, fabricated endorsement and unsupported recommendations. Synthetic production does not reduce the standard for factual claims about real people.

## Scope and ownership

This policy covers generated or materially altered speech, synthetic interview formats, voice models and all excerpts released through lab accounts. It applies to student staff, employees and contractors working for the lab. The faculty director owns the policy. The editor maintains the release register. A researcher who created a script may not be its sole final reviewer.

## Permitted uses

The lab may use licensed stock computer voices for accessible narration of verified retrospective research, clearly labeled classroom demonstrations and translation of approved analysis where a qualified reviewer checks the translated meaning. A synthetic interview may assign fictional interviewer and analyst roles only when the opening identifies both as synthetic roles and does not imply that a real interview occurred.

Any conclusion about a player must be tied to a defined metric, period and source. Hypothetical training scenarios must retain their assumptions and may not be presented as current team plans. Each proposed use still requires the review steps below.

## Prohibited uses

The lab must not:

- Imitate or clone an athlete, coach, public official, outside employee or other third party, even for an otherwise truthful script.
- Generate purported endorsements, quotations, confessions, instructions or interviews that a real person did not provide.
- Use an identifiable minor's synthetic voice or likeness, or create sexual, humiliating, threatening or discriminatory synthetic representations.
- Issue synthetic emergency messages, payment instructions, roster decisions, disciplinary notices or authoritative institutional announcements.
- Convert descriptive statistics into guarantees, attribute causal blame that the evidence cannot support, or conceal generation through a misleading filename, caption or edit.
- Publish a variant or excerpt that has not received its own final approval.

Disclosure does not create an exception to these prohibitions.

## Consent and identity controls

Stock voices must be used under terms permitting the intended distribution. Do not advertise a stock voice as a named person. The only possible exception for a modeled real voice is an adult lab researcher narrating their own approved explanation. It requires written, project-specific approval from the faculty director and the researcher before any sample is collected. This exception never permits speaking as a coach or athlete.

The editor records the consenting person's verified identity, approved script, purpose, audiences, channels, generation tool, storage location, expiry and contact route for revocation. Consent is valid for one project for up to 90 days. A new script, audience, channel or purpose requires renewed consent. Participation must be voluntary; declining cannot affect a course grade, job assignment or access to research opportunities.

Revocation stops new generation and scheduled distribution immediately when received. The editor disables lab-controlled public copies within one working day and requests removal from known downstream recipients. The record must state that removal of all third-party copies cannot be guaranteed. Voice samples and reusable models are deleted within seven days after project closure or revocation, unless a documented incident hold is approved. Retain the minimal consent and release record for one year; restrict it to the editor and director.

## Disclosure at every distribution boundary

Use `AI_generated_synthetic` in the filename. Display “Synthetic audio using computer voices” next to the player, in captions and in the release register. Embed the same statement in available title/comment metadata.

The first spoken sentence must say that the audio is synthetic and that no real coach or player is speaking. Every separately shared excerpt must have its own spoken disclosure. An excerpt may not remove the qualifier needed to interpret a statistic or recommendation. If the format cannot accommodate both the disclosure and the necessary context, decline that format.

The final spoken sentence must direct the audience to the attached methods or preserve the material analytical limitation. A transcript and accessible text version accompany each release. Do not claim that these steps prevent all downstream deception.

## Provenance and version records

For each release, preserve the approved source script, data-source links, input/output hashes, metric definitions, tool/voice versions when available, generation settings, reviewer names, approval date and distribution destinations. Store raw source datasets in the approved research location rather than a public repository when the assignment or source rules prohibit redistribution.

When the tool supports signed content credentials and the lab claims them, validate the final exported file and retain the validation result. Record “not available” when signed credentials are unsupported. An unsigned hash must never be described as proof of speaker identity, consent or truth.

Before release, test one ordinary export or re-encoding path. Check the visible label, spoken disclosure and available metadata on the exact final file. If a required label disappears, reattach it and review the transformed version. A valid signature or a negative detector score cannot substitute for the other checks.

## Review and release

Before production, the researcher submits the purpose, source, script, proposed voices, audience and risk of being mistaken for a real person's statement. The editor checks permitted/prohibited status and any consent record. The faculty director must approve the proposal before synthesis begins.

Before distribution, a second reviewer must verify numerical claims against executable calculations, inspect recommendations for unsupported causal language, listen to the entire final audio, check player-name pronunciation and numerical intelligibility, and test disclosure in the final delivery format. Automated duration and clipping tests supplement this listening review. If listening review cannot be completed, publication is blocked.

The editor records a unique release version, hash, approved transcript and destinations. Keep no more than five unreviewed variants in active production. Each translation, edited excerpt and recompressed release receives a distinct register entry and appropriate review; prior approval does not transfer automatically.

## Incident response

Anyone who discovers misattribution, an incorrect claim, missing disclosure or unauthorized identity use must inform the editor immediately. The editor freezes scheduled releases and preserves a restricted copy of relevant files and logs. Within one working day, the editor removes or corrects lab-controlled copies, posts a clear correction where the original appeared, and contacts any person directly represented or materially affected through the lab's approved process.

The director assesses the scope, assigns follow-up and seeks the institution's relevant privacy, legal or communications review when needed. The team records known destinations, removal requests, corrections and unresolved copies. Do not promise complete erasure. Resume production only after the director approves a documented correction to the process that failed.

## Refusal and review of this policy

Decline production when the source cannot support the script, consent is unclear or pressured, an identity is likely to be misattributed, the audience cannot receive an adequate disclosure, a qualified reviewer is unavailable, or expected harm remains disproportionate to the educational benefit. No deadline or detector result overrides these conditions.

The editor audits the release register monthly against active public files. The faculty director reviews this policy each semester and after a material incident. The audit checks actual file hashes, approvals, accessible transcripts and label survival; it does not treat a signed checklist as proof that the audience understood the release.
